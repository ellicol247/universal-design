Introduction to Fly Fishing

Fly fishing is a type of fishing that uses a lightweight artificial fly to catch fish. Unlike regular fishing, fly fishing uses the weight of the fishing line to cast the fly into the water. It is a popular way to catch trout in rivers and streams.

How Fly Fishing Works

Fly fishing takes practice because casting is different from using a normal fishing rod. The goal is to move the rod back and forward smoothly so the line carries the fly to the water. A good cast helps the fly land naturally without scaring the fish.

Where to Fly Fish

Fly fishing can be done in rivers, lakes, and streams. Colorado is a great place for fly fishing because of its many trout streams. Some popular places near Boulder include Boulder Creek and the South Platte River.

Tips for Beginners

When starting out, it is important to practice casting and learn how to read the water. Trout often stay near rocks and slower moving sections of rivers. It also helps to pay attention to insects around the water when choosing a fly.

Fishing Equipment

There are a few basic pieces of equipment needed to start fly fishing.

  • Fly rod
  • Fly reel
  • Fly line
  • Leader and tippet
  • Artificial flies
  • Waders

A 9-foot, 5-weight fly rod is a common choice for beginners who want to fish for trout.

Types of Flies

There are three main types of flies that are commonly used when fly fishing.

Dry Flies

Dry flies float on top of the water and imitate insects that fish eat at the surface.

Nymphs

Nymphs sink below the surface and imitate insects that live underwater.

Streamers

Streamers imitate small fish and other creatures that larger trout like to eat.
